Why Is Auckland Called the City of Sails?

Auckland is called the City of Sails because of its long sailing tradition, its position between two harbours, and one of the highest rates of boat ownership per person anywhere in the world.

Why is Auckland called the City of Sails?

The nickname comes from how much of Auckland life happens on the water. The city sits on a narrow isthmus with the Waitematā Harbour on one side and the Manukau on the other, giving easy access to the Hauraki Gulf and its sheltered sailing waters. Aucklanders own a lot of boats, and on a fine weekend the harbour fills with sails, which is exactly where the name comes from.

Does Auckland really have that many boats?

Yes. Auckland has long been cited as having one of the highest numbers of boats and yacht club members per capita of any city. The combination of warm-enough weather, sheltered gulf waters and a strong marine industry has made recreational sailing a mainstream pastime rather than a niche one.

What is Auckland's connection to the America's Cup?

Deep. New Zealand has been a major force in the America's Cup, and Auckland has hosted the regatta on its harbour more than once, building out the Viaduct and waterfront for it. The Cup turned the city's sailing culture into a global sporting identity and left behind much of the harbourside development you see today.

Where can you see the sailing?

The Viaduct Harbour and Wynyard Quarter are the easiest places to be among the boats, with marinas, bars and the waterfront promenade. Take a Devonport or gulf island ferry and you will pass yachts on the water. For a sail of your own, harbour cruises and sailing experiences run from the downtown waterfront.
