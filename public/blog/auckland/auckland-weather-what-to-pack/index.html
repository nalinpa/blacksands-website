What's the Weather Like in Auckland? (And What to Pack)

Auckland has a mild, humid climate, with warm summers, cool and wet winters, and weather that can change through the day in any season. It rarely gets extreme and effectively never snows in the city. Whenever you visit, pack layers and a raincoat.

What is the weather like in Auckland?

Mild, humid and changeable. Auckland has a temperate maritime climate, which means warm rather than scorching summers, cool rather than freezing winters, and rain spread across the year rather than a single wet season. The surrounding sea keeps temperatures moderate but adds humidity. You are unlikely to face extremes, but you should expect the weather to shift during a day.

The seasons

Remember the seasons are flipped from the northern hemisphere. Summer, December to February, is warm and humid and the best time for beaches and the gulf. Autumn, March to May, is mild and often settled. Winter, June to August, is cool and wet but rarely cold enough for more than a jacket. Spring, September to November, warms up gradually with changeable weather. None of it is severe by world standards.

Four seasons in one day

New Zealanders joke about getting four seasons in one day, and Auckland lives up to it. A bright morning can turn to a sharp shower and back again, driven by the sea air and the city's position between two harbours. The practical lesson is not to trust a sunny start, and to carry something for rain and something warm even on a fine-looking day. Conditions on the exposed coasts and cones change fastest.

What to pack

Layers and a raincoat, in every season. Bring clothes you can add and shed as the day shifts, plus a waterproof layer for the showers that arrive without much warning. Sun protection matters more than visitors expect, since New Zealand's clear air and thin ozone make the UV strong, so a hat and sunscreen are worth packing even in cooler months. Comfortable shoes round it out for the cones and the walking.
