Auckland

Background and travel basics for Auckland and Tāmaki Makaurau, the city built on a volcanic field between two harbours.

  • Is Auckland Walkable?

    Auckland's central city and waterfront are walkable, but the city sprawls and is hilly, so you'll want buses, trains, ferries or rideshare to reach the wider area.

  • What's the Weather Like in Auckland? (And What to Pack)

    Auckland has a mild, humid climate with warm summers, cool wet winters, and changeable weather year-round. Pack layers and a raincoat whatever the season.

  • Auckland vs Wellington: Which Should You Visit?

    Auckland is bigger, with harbours, volcanoes and beaches. Wellington is compact and walkable, known for the arts, coffee and Te Papa. Here is how to choose, or fit in both.

  • Is Auckland Expensive? A Visitor's Budget Guide

    Auckland is moderately expensive by global standards, but you can control costs by eating in the suburbs, using the free attractions, and not staying on the waterfront.

  • Is Auckland Safe for Tourists?

    Auckland is generally a safe city for tourists, with the usual big-city precautions. Here is what to watch for and how to keep your trip trouble-free.

  • Why Is Auckland Called the City of Sails?

    Auckland is called the City of Sails because of its deep sailing culture, two harbours, and one of the highest rates of boat ownership of any city in the world.

  • What Does “Tāmaki Makaurau” Mean?

    Tāmaki Makaurau is the Māori name for Auckland, usually translated as Tāmaki desired by many. Here is what it means and why the isthmus was so prized.

  • What Is Auckland Known For?

    Auckland is known for its volcanoes, two harbours, sailing culture, Polynesian heritage and food diversity. Here's what defines New Zealand's biggest city.

  • How Many Volcanoes Does Auckland Have?

    Auckland sits on a volcanic field of around 53 volcanoes. Here's what that means, which ones you can still see, and whether they could erupt again.
