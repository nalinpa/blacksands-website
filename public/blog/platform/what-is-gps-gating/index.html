What Is GPS-Gating and Why Use It for Tours?

GPS-gating means content in an app unlocks only when the user physically reaches a set location. For tours it turns passive reading into a reason to actually be there, verifies real visits, and builds a sense of discovery stop by stop.

What is GPS-gating?

GPS-gating is using a device's location to control when content becomes available. The app checks where the user is, and only unlocks a stop's story, audio or reward once they are within range of it. Until then the content stays locked. It ties the digital experience to a physical place, so reaching the spot is what triggers the payoff.

Why use GPS-gating for tours?

Because it changes the behaviour you are designing for. Instead of someone skimming all the content from home, the tour becomes a sequence of places to reach, each unlocking as you arrive. That builds anticipation, rewards the effort of getting there, and keeps people moving through the route in the intended order. It makes the tour an experience rather than a document.

What else can gating be used for?

Verification and gamification. Because the unlock proves someone was physically present, gating can confirm genuine check-ins, which matters for things like trail challenges, passport-style collect-them-all tours, loyalty rewards and event activations. Operators can build journeys where finishing unlocks a prize or certificate, knowing the completions are real.

Does gating need a connection to work?

Not if the app is built offline-first. The location check happens on the device using its GPS, which works without mobile data. Combined with content stored locally, that means gating can function in remote areas with no signal, unlocking stops as the user arrives even deep off the grid. The two ideas work best together.
