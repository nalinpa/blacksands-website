Lord of the Rings vs The Hobbit Filming Locations: What's the Difference?

Both the Lord of the Rings and The Hobbit trilogies were filmed entirely in New Zealand and share many of the same locations. The key differences are when they were filmed, a few Hobbit-only spots like Lake Pukaki, and that Hobbiton was rebuilt as a permanent set for The Hobbit.

What's the difference between the LOTR and Hobbit locations?

Mostly timing and overlap rather than two separate maps. The Lord of the Rings trilogy was filmed around 1999 to 2000, with pickups to 2003. The Hobbit trilogy was filmed between 2011 and 2013, reusing many of the same landscapes and adding some new ones. So a Middle-earth trip covers both at once, since the regions are largely shared.

Why is Hobbiton different between the two?

Because it was built twice. For the Lord of the Rings the Hobbiton set was temporary and largely removed after filming. For The Hobbit it was rebuilt with permanent materials, which is why the version you visit today survives as a complete, maintained set. Hobbiton is the one place where you can still walk through original standing sets rather than just the landscape.

Which locations are unique to The Hobbit?

A handful of South Island spots feature more in The Hobbit. Lake Pukaki, with its bright turquoise water and views of Aoraki Mount Cook, was used as Laketown in The Desolation of Smaug. The Pelorus River near Nelson was the barrel-escape scene. Some of these can be visited or even kayaked, and the wider Mackenzie Country is worth seeing regardless of the film link.

Are the shared locations recognisable from both?

Often yes, since the films leaned on the same dramatic landscapes. Tongariro National Park, Fiordland and the Queenstown and Glenorchy area appear across both trilogies. Some scenes were heavily altered with digital effects and are hard to place in person, while the big landscape shots are usually still clearly recognisable. Hobbiton aside, you are mostly visiting scenery, not standing sets.
