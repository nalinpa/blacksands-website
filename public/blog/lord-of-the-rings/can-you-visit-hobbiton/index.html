Can You Visit Hobbiton and How Much Does It Cost?

Yes, you can visit Hobbiton. It is near Matamata in the Waikato, open daily except Christmas Day, and accessible only on a guided tour. From April 2026 the standard tour costs 130 NZD per adult.

Can you visit Hobbiton?

Yes, but only on a guided tour. The set sits on the Alexander family's working sheep and beef farm and is not open to walk around on your own. The standard Signature Tour runs about two and a half hours and takes you through the 12-acre set, past the 44 hobbit holes and Bag End, into the interior of a hobbit home on Bagshot Row, and finishes with a complimentary drink at the Green Dragon Inn. Walk-ins are almost never possible, so you need to book.

How much does a Hobbiton tour cost?

From April 2026 the standard Signature Tour is 130 NZD per adult and 65 NZD per youth aged 11 to 17. A family pass for two adults and two youth is 332 NZD. Children under 10 are free but still need a ticket booked in advance. If you want to make more of a day of it, the tour and lunch combo with a meal in the Party Marquee runs around 170 NZD per adult, and there are pricier evening banquet and behind-the-scenes options.

How do you get to Hobbiton from Auckland?

Hobbiton is about two to two and a half hours south of Auckland. The simplest option is to self-drive to The Shire's Rest at 501 Buckland Road, where parking is free and there are two EV charging stations. You can also take an InterCity bus, or book a day tour with return transport from Auckland if you would rather not drive. Many people pick Hobbiton up on the way through to Rotorua, which is about 45 minutes further on.

How far ahead should you book?

Book weeks ahead, and earlier again for the summer peak from December to February, when tours regularly sell out. Dates open roughly 10 months in advance. If your preferred slot shows as fully booked, keep checking the official site, since cancellations are released in real time. Always book through hobbitontours.com to avoid look-alike sites.

Is Hobbiton worth it if you're not a fan?

For most people, yes. Even setting the films aside, the craft is the draw. The set is permanent and meticulously maintained, down to fake trees built for the right look and gardens kept in bloom. It reads less like a theme park and more like a film location frozen in time, which is exactly what it is.
