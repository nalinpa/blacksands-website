Every Lord of the Rings Scene You Can Stand In

Many of the actual filmed moments from the Lord of the Rings are real places you can stand in today, from the field where the hobbits hid from the Black Rider to the hill that became Edoras. They are spread across both islands.

Can you really stand where the scenes were filmed?

Yes, for a surprising number of them. The trilogy was shot across more than 150 real New Zealand locations, and while many sets were removed and some scenes were heavily digital, the landscapes remain. At a lot of spots you can stand on the exact ground where a scene was filmed, from famous moments at Hobbiton and Edoras to smaller ones like the wooded bank where Frodo first hides from a Black Rider. Hobbiton is the one place where original sets still stand.

North Island scenes

The North Island holds the start of the journey. The Shire was filmed at Hobbiton near Matamata, the only location with standing sets. Mordor and the slopes of Mount Doom are in Tongariro National Park. Around Wellington, Mount Victoria's town belt was used for the Black Rider and the woods near the edge of the Shire, and Kaitoke Regional Park became Rivendell. These cluster well for a few days.

South Island scenes

The South Island carries the epic landscapes. Edoras was built on Mount Sunday in Canterbury, the Battle of the Pelennor Fields was filmed near Twizel, and the Glenorchy and Queenstown area stood in for Lothlórien, Isengard and the river country of the Anduin. These locations are more remote and spread out, and the scenery is spectacular in its own right, film connection aside.

How do you find the exact spots?

Some are signposted, many are not. The famous locations like Hobbiton and Edoras are easy to find, but the scene-level spots, the exact tree, bank or ridge where a moment was shot, often sit unmarked in a wider landscape. A dedicated location guide that maps each scene to its real-world point is the way to find them on the ground, especially the smaller moments that no signpost will lead you to.
