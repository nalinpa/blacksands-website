Where Was Lord of the Rings Filmed in New Zealand?

The Lord of the Rings was filmed entirely in New Zealand, across more than 150 locations on both islands. The headline sites are Hobbiton near Matamata, Tongariro National Park for Mordor, Mount Sunday for Edoras, and the fields near Twizel for the great battle.

Where was Lord of the Rings filmed in New Zealand?

Peter Jackson grew up near Wellington and used the whole country as Middle-earth. The locations most fans want to see are spread across both islands.

  • Hobbiton — Matamata, Waikato. The Shire, built on a working farm and rebuilt for the Hobbit films. Now a permanent attraction.
  • Mordor and Mount Doom — Tongariro National Park. Mount Ngauruhoe stood in for Mount Doom, digitally altered for the films.
  • Edoras — Mount Sunday, Canterbury. A lone hill rising from the Rangitata Valley, ringed by the Southern Alps. The set was fully removed after filming.
  • Paths of the Dead — Putangirua Pinnacles, near Wellington. An eerie badland of eroded rock spires, reachable on a short walk.
  • Battle of the Pelennor Fields — near Twizel, Mackenzie Country. The largest single location used in the films, on private farmland.
  • Isengard and Rivendell foreground — Kaitoke Regional Park, near Wellington. A replica Elven archway marks the Rivendell site.

Can you visit the filming locations?

Yes, around 30 to 40 of them welcome visitors. Many are free and self-drive accessible, including Tongariro National Park, Mount Sunday, Mount Victoria in Wellington and Kaitoke Regional Park. A few sit on private land and can only be seen on a guided tour, most notably Hobbiton and the Pelennor Fields near Twizel. The Mines of Moria and most interiors were studio or digital work with no real-world site.

Where is Hobbiton and how do you visit it?

Hobbiton sits on a sheep and beef farm near Matamata, about two hours south of Auckland. It is only accessible on a guided tour, which walks you through the set, past Bag End, and finishes with a drink at the Green Dragon Inn. Book ahead in summer, when daily tours fill up.

What was used as Mount Doom?

Mount Ngauruhoe, the near-perfect volcanic cone in Tongariro National Park, was used for Mount Doom and altered in post-production. The best way to see it is the Tongariro Alpine Crossing, a one-day alpine hike of around 19 to 20 kilometres that passes its base. It is a serious walk in changeable mountain weather, so check the forecast and go prepared.

How long do you need to see them all?

To cover both islands properly, plan 10 to 14 days. A North Island run taking in Hobbiton and Tongariro works in 4 to 5 days. The South Island needs 6 to 8 days to reach Edoras, Glenorchy and Twizel comfortably. Download offline maps before the rural drives, where mobile signal drops out for long stretches.
