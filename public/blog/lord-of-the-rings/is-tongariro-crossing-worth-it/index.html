Is the Tongariro Crossing Worth It and How Hard Is It?

The Tongariro Alpine Crossing is worth it, often called the best day hike in New Zealand, and it is rated hard. It runs about 20 kilometres with around 800 metres of climbing and takes most people 6 to 8 hours. It is not technical, but it is a long, exposed alpine day.

Is the Tongariro Crossing worth it?

For most people who are fit enough, yes. It crosses an active volcanic landscape of old lava flows, steaming vents, the Red Crater and the vivid Emerald Lakes, with Mount Ngauruhoe, the stand-in for Mount Doom, alongside the route. It is widely rated one of the great day walks in the world. The scenery is unlike anywhere else in the country, which is exactly why it earns the reputation.

How hard is it?

Rated hard, mainly because of length and exposure rather than technical difficulty. You cover around 20 kilometres with roughly 800 metres of climbing over 6 to 8 hours, including the steep Devil's Staircase and a loose scree descent from the Red Crater that is tough on knees. No ropes or scrambling are needed, but you need real endurance and reasonable fitness. Most reasonably active people finish it, and feel it the next day.

What do you need to know before you go?

Weather is the big one. The crossing is high and exposed, the temperature at the top can be 10 to 15 degrees colder than the base, and conditions change fast, so warm layers, a raincoat, sun protection, and a full day's food and water are essential. It is a point-to-point walk, so you need a shuttle, usually around 50 to 60 dollars, since parking at the ends is restricted. Start early.

When is the best time to do it?

Summer, roughly November to April, is the safest window for walking it unguided. Outside that, snow and ice make it a mountaineering proposition that needs proper alpine skills and gear. Even in summer, check the forecast the day before and be willing to turn back. The local iwi ask that visitors do not climb the sacred summit of Mount Ngauruhoe, so stick to the main crossing.
