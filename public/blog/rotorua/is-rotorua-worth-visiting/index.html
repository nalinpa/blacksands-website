Is Rotorua Worth Visiting?

Yes, Rotorua is worth visiting for almost anyone travelling around New Zealand's North Island. It is the one place in the country where you can see active geysers, boiling mud and steaming ground in the middle of a city, experience Māori culture where it is most visible day to day, and soak in natural hot pools, all within a short drive of each other. The main downsides are the sulphur smell, the cost of the paid attractions adding up, and some cultural experiences feeling built for large tour groups, but none of these outweigh what Rotorua offers for a stay of two days or more.

Why Rotorua is worth it

Geothermal activity you can't see elsewhere in a city

Rotorua sits on an active geothermal field, and it shows everywhere. At Waiotapu Thermal Wonderland, the Lady Knox Geyser erupts daily at 10:15am, a short walk from the Champagne Pool and the Artist's Palette. At Te Puia, the Pohutu Geyser erupts several times a day. Even without paying for entry, Kuirau Park on the edge of the city centre has steaming lakes, bubbling mud and free public foot baths.

Living Māori culture

In the 2023 census, 46% of Rotorua's population identified as Māori, the highest proportion of any city in New Zealand, and the culture is part of everyday life here rather than something staged for visitors. At Whakarewarewa, the Tūhourangi Ngāti Wāhiao people have welcomed visitors to their living village for more than two centuries. Evening experiences at Mitai Māori Village and Te Pā Tū combine kapa haka performance with a hāngī dinner cooked in an earth oven.

Hot pools for every budget

Options range from the lakefront Polynesian Spa and Wai Ariki Hot Springs to the mud baths at Hells Gate, and free natural soaks at Kerosene Creek and Hot 'n' Cold, where a hot thermal stream meets a cold one.

Adventure

Rotorua is often called the adventure capital of the North Island. Whakarewarewa Forest has more than 100km of mountain bike trails through redwood and native forest, free to ride. The Kaituna River at Okere Falls has 14 rapids, including Tutea Falls, which at 7m is the highest commercially rafted waterfall in the world. Add the Skyline luge, ziplines, the Redwoods Treewalk and zorbing, and there is more to do than most visitors have time for.

Easy to reach

Rotorua is just under three hours' drive from Auckland and about an hour from both Tauranga and Taupō, so it slots easily into a North Island road trip.

The honest downsides

The smell

Rotorua smells of sulphur, often compared to rotten eggs, caused by hydrogen sulphide from the geothermal activity. It is stronger in some parts of town than others and varies with the weather. Most visitors get used to it within a day, and some choose accommodation outside the city centre to avoid it.

The cost of paid attractions

Geothermal parks, cultural evenings and adventure activities each carry their own entry fee, and doing several in one trip adds up quickly. The fix is to pick one or two headline attractions and fill the rest of your time with Rotorua's free geothermal sights, walks, glow worms and hot pools.

Some experiences feel built for tour groups

The standard evening format of kapa haka plus hāngī is designed to host large numbers of visitors, which will not suit everyone, although it still gives a real window into Māori culture. If you want more depth, look for smaller-group experiences, or spend time at Ohinemutu, a lakeside village that is still home to a Māori community.

You'll want a car

Many of the best sights, including Waiotapu, Waimangu and the free hot pools, are 20 to 35 minutes south of the city, and Okere Falls is north east of town. Local buses reach Te Puia and the Redwoods, and the city centre is walkable, but a car makes the trip far easier.

Some things have changed

The Rotorua Museum building in Government Gardens has finished its earthquake strengthening but is still closed while exhibitions are developed, with a full public opening planned for 2028. Hamurana Springs, once free, now charges entry. Older guides may not reflect either change.

Rotorua vs a day trip

Rotorua works as a day trip from Tauranga or Taupō, but from Auckland it means close to six hours of driving for one day of sightseeing. Staying at least one night lets you add a cultural evening, glow worms and, from October to February, the Thursday Night Market, which are among the best parts of a visit.

When to go

There is no bad time to visit, since the geothermal parks, geysers and hot pools run year round. Late February to early April offers the best balance of warm weather and smaller crowds, while winter is quieter and ideal for hot pools.

The verdict

Rotorua is worth visiting. Few places anywhere combine active geothermal landscapes, a strong living Māori culture and this much adventure in one compact area. Give it at least two days, choose your paid attractions carefully, and use the free geothermal sights and hot pools to fill the gaps.
