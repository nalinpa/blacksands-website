Ultimate Guide to Rotorua's Thermal Parks

Rotorua has five main paid geothermal parks: Waiotapu Thermal Wonderland, Waimangu Volcanic Valley, Te Puia, Whakarewarewa Living Village and Hells Gate. It also has several free geothermal areas, including Kuirau Park, Sulphur Point, the Waiotapu Mud Pools and Ohinemutu. The parks fall into three clusters: south of the city on State Highway 5, in the city itself, and north east of town. For a first visit, one or two paid parks plus the free areas is enough to see geysers, boiling mud, hot waterfalls and colourful mineral pools.

South of the city: State Highway 5

Waiotapu Thermal Wonderland

About 30 minutes south of Rotorua, Waiotapu is the most colourful geothermal park in the region. Well-formed trails pass the Champagne Pool, a steaming pool with a bright orange rim, and the Artist's Palette, a spread of pools and mineral deposits in yellows, greens and oranges. The shortest loop takes about 45 minutes and the full 3km circuit about 90.

The Lady Knox Geyser erupts once a day at 10:15am at a separate site about 2km from the main entrance. Staff trigger it with a soap-like surfactant, so it runs to time. Arrive by 9:45am to get a viewing spot, and plan the rest of your morning around it. Adults are $45 and children aged 5 to 15 are $15.

Best for: colour, photography, and pairing with the free hot pools nearby.

Waiotapu Mud Pools

Just off Waiotapu Loop Road, the Waiotapu Mud Pools are free to view from a boardwalk platform. Expect bubbling grey mud and a strong sulphur smell. It is a quick stop, easy to add to a Waiotapu visit or a soak at Hot 'n' Cold on the same road.

Waimangu Volcanic Valley

Waimangu is the world's youngest geothermal system, formed by the 1886 eruption of Mt Tarawera. Rather than a loop, the visit is a mostly downhill walk of 1 to 2 hours through a regenerating valley, past Frying Pan Lake and the pale blue Inferno Crater Lake.

Complimentary shuttle buses run from stops along the valley back up to the visitor centre, so you only have to walk downhill and can finish wherever suits you. At the bottom, you can add a 45-minute guided cruise on Lake Rotomahana. Cruise places are limited, so book ahead in summer, and note that last admission is 1:30pm.

Best for: walkers and anyone interested in the Tarawera eruption.

In the city: Whakarewarewa and the free parks

Te Puia

Te Puia covers 63 hectares of the Whakarewarewa geothermal valley at 20 Hemo Road, just south of the city centre. It is home to the Pohutu Geyser, which erupts several times a day, plus a Kiwi Conservation Centre, Rotowhio Marae, and the New Zealand Māori Arts and Crafts Institute, where you can watch carvers and weavers in training. Allow around three hours. The guided Te Rā experience is $105 per adult and $55.50 per child. Local buses stop nearby, so you do not need a car.

Best for: seeing a natural geyser, Māori arts, kiwi, and visitors without a car.

Whakarewarewa Living Village

On the other side of the same valley, Whakarewarewa describes itself as The Living Māori Village. The Tūhourangi Ngāti Wāhiao people have welcomed visitors here for more than two centuries, and residents still use the geothermal steam and pools for cooking. Guided tours of about an hour run through the day and cost $48 per adult and $22 per child, with cultural performances of haka and poi, and food cooked in the ground, available at extra cost.

Best for: seeing how a community lives alongside geothermal activity.

Pohaturoa Track (free Pohutu Geyser lookout)

The Pohaturoa Track is a free 3.7km return walk to a public viewpoint looking across the Whakarewarewa valley at the Pohutu Geyser. The view is more distant than from inside Te Puia, but it is the only way to see Pohutu erupt without paying.

Kuirau Park

Kuirau Park is a free, always open thermal park on the edge of the CBD. Paths lead past a steaming lake, boiling pools and bubbling mud, most of it fenced off just metres from the footpath. The park also has free public foot baths, a playground, paddling pools and barbecues, and a market every Saturday from 7am to 1pm.

Best for: a free geothermal walk in town, families, and a quick foot soak.

Sulphur Point

Sulphur Point is a free geothermal walking and cycling area on the lakefront beside Government Gardens, with steaming ground right beside the path. It pairs well with a walk through the gardens and along the lake.

Ohinemutu

Ohinemutu is a lakeside village that is still home to a Māori community, with steam rising between the houses and St Faith's Anglican Church at its centre. It is free to walk through, but it is a living community rather than an attraction, so visit quietly and respectfully.

North east of town

Hells Gate

Hells Gate, known to local iwi Ngāti Rangiteaorere as Tikitere, is the most geothermally active reserve in the Rotorua district, and it is Māori owned and run by Ngāti Rangiteaorere. A 2.5km boardwalk loop, taking about 90 minutes, passes boiling pools, steaming cliffs and a mud volcano. The standout is Kakahi Falls, the largest hot waterfall in the Southern Hemisphere at around 40°C, traditionally used by Māori warriors to bathe and heal wounds after battle. The geothermal walk is from $45 per adult.

The on-site Wai Ora Spa offers mud baths and sulphur pools, booked separately from the geothermal walk. Hells Gate is open daily from 8:30am to 10pm, closed Christmas Day, which makes it the only major thermal park you can visit in the evening.

Best for: the most active geothermal features, mud baths, and evening visits.

Thermal pools for soaking

The parks above are mostly for looking. For soaking, Rotorua's options range from the lakefront Polynesian Spa and Wai Ariki Hot Springs to Waikite Valley Thermal Pools, fed by Te Manaroa Spring, and the free natural pools at Kerosene Creek and Hot 'n' Cold.

How to plan your visits

  • Group by area. Waiotapu and Waimangu are both on the southern trip, Te Puia and the free city parks make another half day, and Hells Gate works on its own or on the way to Okere Falls.
  • Start south early. Waiotapu's Lady Knox Geyser erupts at 10:15am, so that trip sets the start time for your day. If you want Waimangu on the same day, watch its 1:30pm last admission, and allow over three hours if you add the cruise. Doing both properly in one day is tight.
  • Don't overdo it. Most visitors find two paid parks is plenty. Pick parks that differ, such as Waiotapu for colour and Te Puia for geysers and culture, rather than two similar ones.
  • Fill gaps for free. Kuirau Park, Sulphur Point and the Waiotapu Mud Pools cost nothing and take less than an hour each.

Staying safe in geothermal areas

  • Stay on marked paths and boardwalks. Water and ground beside the paths can be boiling, and the crust over some areas is thin.
  • Keep children close, especially near unfenced edges.
  • Never touch the water or mud in geothermal parks.
  • Wear closed shoes for walking.
  • Take extra care after dark in free areas like Kuirau Park, where lighting is limited.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best thermal park in Rotorua?

For most first-time visitors with a car, Waiotapu, because of its colour and the Lady Knox Geyser. Without a car, Te Puia.

Can you see a geyser in Rotorua for free?

Yes. The Pohaturoa Track leads to a free public lookout over the Pohutu Geyser at Whakarewarewa.

Which Rotorua thermal park is open latest?

Hells Gate, open until 10pm daily.

Which thermal parks are Māori owned or run?

Hells Gate is owned by Ngāti Rangiteaorere, and Whakarewarewa Living Village is home to the Tūhourangi Ngāti Wāhiao people. Te Puia is home to the New Zealand Māori Arts and Crafts Institute.

Are Rotorua's thermal parks accessible?

Partly, and it varies a lot by park:

  • Te Puia is the most accessible. Most paths are paved or compacted gravel, the carving and weaving schools are fully accessible, and the Pohutu Geyser can be seen from accessible platforms, although some inclines near the geyser may need assistance.
  • Waiotapu has wheelchair-friendly boardwalks and platforms past the Champagne Pool and Artist's Palette, but some sections are steep and one has stairs. A power chair or a strong helper makes a big difference. Accessible parking is near the entrance.
  • Waimangu has flat sections staff will help you reach, but the Inferno Crater track is unsuitable for wheelchairs. The shuttle buses have no lifts, so wheelchairs need to be lightweight and foldable, and motorised chairs and scooters cannot be carried.
  • Hells Gate tracks are built from natural stone, so it recommends only off-road wheelchairs with an experienced helper. The spa itself is wheelchair accessible.
  • Kuirau Park and Sulphur Point are flat and free, and the easiest geothermal areas to reach with a wheelchair or pushchair.

For Whakarewarewa Living Village, and for any specific needs, call ahead before you go.
