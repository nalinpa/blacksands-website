1 Day in Rotorua: The Perfect Itinerary

With one day in Rotorua, spend the morning at Waiotapu Thermal Wonderland to see the Lady Knox Geyser erupt at 10:15am, stop at the free Waiotapu Mud Pools on the way back, have lunch on Eat Streat, walk the Redwoods Treewalk in the afternoon, soak at the Polynesian Spa, and finish with a hāngī dinner and glow worm walk at Mitai Māori Village. That covers Rotorua's four essentials in one day: geothermal activity, a hot pool soak, the Redwoods and Māori culture. You will need a car for the morning, since Waiotapu is about 30 minutes south of the city.

Morning: Waiotapu and the Lady Knox Geyser

Start the day at Waiotapu Thermal Wonderland, about 30 minutes south of Rotorua on State Highway 5. It is the most colourful of Rotorua's geothermal parks, and the timing matters. The Lady Knox Geyser erupts once a day at 10:15am, and the park recommends arriving by 9:45am to find a viewing spot before it goes off.

After the eruption, walk the park's trails past the Champagne Pool and the Artist's Palette, the two features most visitors come for. The paths are well formed throughout.

On the way out, stop at the Waiotapu Mud Pools just off Waiotapu Loop Road. They are free to view and take only a few minutes.

Optional soak: if you have packed togs and a towel, Hot 'n' Cold is on the same road. It is a free spot where a hot thermal stream meets a cold one, so you can move to a temperature that suits you. There are no toilets or changing rooms, and keep your head above the water, since untreated geothermal water carries a small risk of amoebic meningitis. If you soak here, you can skip the Polynesian Spa later and give that time to something else.

Lunch: Eat Streat

Drive back into Rotorua for lunch on Eat Streat, the pedestrian-only strip at the lake end of Tutanekai Street. It is covered by a retractable roof with geothermally heated footpaths, and the restaurants and bars range from Indian and Thai to Mexican and pub food. For something quicker and cheaper, Patrick's Pies Gold Star Bakery on Old Taupo Road is an award-winning pie bakery.

Afternoon: the Redwoods

Spend the afternoon in Whakarewarewa Forest on the south east edge of town. The Redwoods Treewalk crosses 28 suspension bridges through the redwood canopy and takes about 40 minutes. It suits anyone who can walk unaided, and free strollers are available. If you would rather not pay, the free Redwoods Circuit is a 3.4km loop through the same forest, past tree ferns and a small blue spring.

Late afternoon: a soak at the Polynesian Spa

Before dinner, soak at the Polynesian Spa, a long-running mineral pool complex on the lakefront within walking distance of the city centre. It is the easiest place to finish the day's geothermal theme with a proper soak and changing facilities. The pools are open until 11pm, so if you run late you can always come back after dinner instead.

Evening: Mitai Māori Village

End the day at Mitai Māori Village for a haka performance, a hāngī dinner cooked in an earth oven, and a glow worm stream walk on site. It packs two of Rotorua's night highlights into one booking. The evening starts at 6pm, or 6:30pm in summer, and runs about three hours, with hotel pickups available. Book in advance, since places fill quickly.

Alternative: Te Pā Tū, formerly Tamaki Māori Village, offers storytelling, haka and a hāngī in a forest village setting, with return transport from town. Sessions start at 5pm and 6:15pm, plus 7:30pm from October to April.

Variations on the one-day plan

If you don't have a car

Swap Waiotapu for Te Puia, home to the Pohutu Geyser and the Māori Arts and Crafts Institute. Plan on around three hours there. Local buses serve both Te Puia and the Redwoods, and the city centre is walkable, so the rest of the day works without a car. Mitai and Te Pā Tū both offer transport from town. Add a free walk through Kuirau Park and its foot baths on the edge of the CBD.

If you want adventure

Replace the Redwoods with an afternoon at Skyline Rotorua, where the gondola climbs Mt Ngongotaha to the luge track and Sky Swing. Skyline and Mitai Māori Village are both on Fairy Springs Road, so it is an easy move from the luge to dinner. For rafting, the Kaituna River at Okere Falls, about 20km north east of the city, has 14 rapids including Tutea Falls, the highest commercially rafted waterfall in the world at 7m. A rafting trip takes up most of an afternoon, so drop the Polynesian Spa if you add it.

If you're on a budget

Keep the free parts of the day and swap the rest:

  • Waiotapu Mud Pools and Hot 'n' Cold instead of the paid park
  • The Pohaturoa Track, a free 3.7km return walk to a public lookout over the Pohutu Geyser
  • The Redwoods Circuit instead of the Treewalk
  • The Te Auheke Track after dark for free glow worms at the Cascade Falls glow worm dell

If it's a Thursday

Between October and February, swap dinner for the Rotorua Night Market, which runs from 5pm to 9pm on Tutanekai Street, weather permitting, with food trucks, craft stalls and live music.

If it's raining

Eat Streat is covered, and hot pools are just as good in the rain. Swap the Redwoods for a longer soak at the Polynesian Spa, or spend the evening at Hells Gate, which is open until 10pm and has mud baths and sulphur pools at its on-site spa.

Is one day enough for Rotorua?

One day covers the highlights if you plan around the Lady Knox Geyser and book your evening in advance. Two nights is a more comfortable minimum for one geothermal park, a soak and an adventure activity, so if you can stay longer, split this itinerary over two days and add the lakes and a longer walk.
