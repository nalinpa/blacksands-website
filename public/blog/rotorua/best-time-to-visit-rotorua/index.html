Best Time to Visit Rotorua

The best time to visit Rotorua is from late February to early April, when the weather is still warm, the summer school holidays have ended, and the main attractions are quieter than in peak summer. December to February is the warmest and busiest period, best for lake swimming and outdoor events. Winter is cold and wet but has the fewest crowds, and it is the best season for hot pools. The geothermal parks, geysers, hot pools and cultural experiences run year round, so there is no bad time to visit, only different trade-offs.

Rotorua has a temperate climate with distinct seasons. Daytime highs average around 23°C in February and 12°C in July, with overnight lows dropping from about 13°C in February to under 4°C in July. Rain falls throughout the year, totalling around 1,350mm annually. The wettest stretch runs from April through August, peaking in July, while October and November are the driest months. Pack a rain jacket whatever the season.

Summer (December to February): warmest and busiest

Summer brings Rotorua's warmest, longest days, with around 15 hours of daylight in December. It is the best season for swimming in the lakes, including Blue Lake (Lake Tikitapu), walking the Tarawera Trail, and outdoor dining on Eat Streat. The Thursday Night Market on Tutanekai Street also runs through the warmer months, roughly October to February.

It is also the busiest time. New Zealand's summer school holidays run from mid-December to late January or early February, so domestic visitor numbers peak then. Several major events also fall in this window:

  • Blue Lake Multisport Festival: Auckland Anniversary weekend, next 30 to 31 January 2027. Auckland Anniversary Day is a public holiday in Rotorua too.
  • Waitangi Day: 6 February, with a free official celebration on the lakefront.
  • Tarawera Ultra-Trail: next 13 to 14 February 2027.

Best for: swimming, walking, long days, events.
Watch out for: peak accommodation demand during school holidays and event weekends. Book early.

Autumn (March to May): the sweet spot

Early autumn is the best balance of weather and crowds. March is still warm, with daytime highs around 21°C, and with schools back from late January or early February, crowds are noticeably down on peak summer. April and May are cooler and noticeably wetter, so aim for late February and March if you want the warm, settled version of autumn.

Autumn events:

  • Crankworx Rotorua: mountain bike festival at Skyline Rotorua, next 10 to 14 March 2027.
  • Rotorua Marathon: a 42.2km lap of Lake Rotorua, next 1 May 2027.
  • Highlander MTB Challenge: from Te Puia through Whakarewarewa Forest to Lake Tikitapu, next 30 May 2027.

Avoid Easter and the April school holidays if you want the quietest version of autumn. In 2027, Good Friday is 26 March and Easter Monday is 29 March, and the April school holidays run from 10 to 26 April.

Best for: mild weather without peak crowds, mountain biking, walking.
Watch out for: Crankworx week and Easter, when accommodation fills, and wetter weather from April.

Winter (June to August): cold, quiet and best for hot pools

Winter is Rotorua's coldest and wettest season, with daytime highs around 12 to 13°C, frosty nights and the shortest days, with under 10 hours of daylight in June. It is also the quietest time outside the July school holidays, which in 2027 run from 3 to 18 July.

Winter suits Rotorua better than most destinations. A soak at the Polynesian Spa, Hells Gate or a free hot stream feels best on a cold day, and steam from the geothermal parks and Kuirau Park is much more visible in cold air. Eat Streat is covered, with geothermally heated footpaths, so evenings out still work in the rain.

Winter events:

  • Matariki: the Māori New Year, with a public holiday on Friday 25 June 2027, marked by celebrations around Rotorua.
  • King's Birthday weekend: Monday 7 June 2027, a public holiday that brings a busy long weekend.

Best for: hot pools, fewer crowds, lower demand for accommodation, Matariki.
Watch out for: rain, cold mornings and short days. Plan outdoor walks for the middle of the day.

Spring (September to November): warming up

Spring starts cool, with highs around 15°C in September, and warms steadily to around 19°C by November as the days lengthen. It also dries out as it goes, and October and November are the driest months of the year. Crowds stay moderate apart from the spring school holidays, which run from 25 September to 10 October in 2027.

November is the pick of spring: warmer, longer days, the lowest rainfall of the year, and still ahead of the summer rush.

Best for: shoulder-season travel, especially November.
Watch out for: cool mornings and changeable weather in September.

What doesn't change with the seasons

Most of Rotorua's headline experiences run year round:

  • The geothermal parks, including Waiotapu Thermal Wonderland, Te Puia, Hells Gate and Waimangu
  • The Lady Knox Geyser, which erupts daily at 10:15am
  • Hot pools, both commercial and free
  • Māori cultural evenings at Mitai Māori Village and Te Pā Tū
  • The Redwoods Treewalk, by day and after dark
  • Glow worms on forest tracks like the Te Auheke Track

The main exception is the Rotorua Night Market, which runs Thursdays from 5pm to 9pm roughly October through February, weather permitting.
