What Should a First-Time Visitor Do in Auckland?

Climb a volcano for the view, take a ferry to an island, eat in the suburbs rather than the waterfront, and use the city as a base for a day trip or two. That is the shape of a good first visit to Auckland.

What should a first-time visitor do in Auckland?

Auckland rewards getting up high, out on the water, and into the suburbs. A strong first visit covers all three.

  • Climb a cone. Maungawhau (Mount Eden) gives a 360-degree view over the whole isthmus in a 20-minute walk. See the best cones to walk.
  • Take a ferry. Rangitoto for a volcanic summit walk, or Waiheke for beaches and vineyards. Both leave from downtown. Here is how the Rangitoto walk works.
  • Eat in the suburbs. Dominion Road and Sandringham Road beat the waterfront for value and flavour. See where locals eat and the best cheap eats.
  • Walk the waterfront. Wynyard Quarter and the Viaduct are easy, free and central.
  • Take a day trip. West coast black-sand beaches like Piha, or Hobbiton two hours south.

How many days do you need in Auckland?

Two to three days covers the city itself comfortably: a cone, an island, the food and the waterfront. Add a day for each big day trip you want, whether that is Waiheke, the west coast beaches, or Hobbiton. Many people use Auckland as the start of a longer North Island trip rather than the whole holiday.

What is the best free thing to do?

Walk up a volcano. Mount Eden and One Tree Hill both give you the big view for nothing, and the climb is short. Beyond that, the waterfront, the Domain parkland and the west coast beaches all cost nothing to enjoy. Auckland's best moments are often outdoors and free.

How do you get around?

The city centre, waterfront and nearest cones are walkable. For the suburbs and ferries, use an AT HOP card on buses, trains and ferries, or rideshare for the shorter hops. A car is most useful for the west coast beaches and day trips out of the city, less so in the centre where parking is tight.
