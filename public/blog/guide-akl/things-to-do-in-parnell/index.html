Things to Do in Parnell: Auckland's Oldest Suburb

Parnell is one of Auckland's oldest suburbs, known for its heritage village, the Rose Gardens, galleries and its closeness to the Auckland Domain and museum. It is an easy, characterful corner of the city to spend half a day.

What is there to do in Parnell?

Parnell mixes history, gardens and easy walking. The suburb grew up early in Auckland's life, so it carries heritage buildings, a village strip of shops and cafes, and a more refined feel than the busier centre. Add the Rose Gardens, the edge of the Auckland Domain and the museum nearby, and it makes a relaxed half-day away from the downtown bustle.

The Parnell Rose Gardens

The Rose Gardens are the suburb's signature. Set on the slope toward the harbour, they hold a large collection of roses alongside lawns and mature trees, at their best in late spring and summer when the roses are in bloom. They are free to wander and a genuinely pretty spot, popular for a stroll, a picnic or photos, with harbour glimpses through the planting.

Parnell village and beyond

The village strip is the other half of a Parnell visit. Its heritage buildings hold boutiques, galleries, cafes and restaurants, making it a pleasant place to browse and eat. The suburb sits within easy reach of the Auckland Domain, the city's oldest park and a volcanic crater, and the Auckland War Memorial Museum on its rise, so you can easily combine the two.

How to visit Parnell

Parnell is a short distance from the city centre, easy to reach by bus, rideshare or a walk through the Domain. Treat it as a half-day, browsing the village, walking the Rose Gardens, and adding the Domain and museum next door. It pairs naturally with the waterfront, since the suburb runs down toward the harbour on the city's eastern side.
