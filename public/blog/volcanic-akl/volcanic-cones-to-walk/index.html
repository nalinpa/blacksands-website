Best Volcanic Cones to Walk in Auckland

Auckland is built on a volcanic field of around 53 volcanoes, and several of the cones are walkable in under half an hour. The best to walk are Maungawhau (Mount Eden), Maungakiekie (One Tree Hill), Mount Victoria and North Head in Devonport, and Mount Wellington.

What volcanic cones can you walk in Auckland?

Five cones stand out for easy access and strong views. Each has a formed track to the summit and free public access. Most summits are now foot-access only, since vehicle access to several maunga was closed to protect them.

  • Maungawhau (Mount Eden) — the highest natural point on the isthmus at 196 metres, with a 360-degree view and a deep grassed crater. Roughly 15 to 20 minutes up on foot.
  • Maungakiekie (One Tree Hill) — a large cone in Cornwall Park with an obelisk at the summit and grazing sheep on the slopes. A gentle longer walk through parkland.
  • Mount Victoria (Takarunga) — the highest point on the North Shore, a short climb above Devonport village with harbour views.
  • North Head (Maungauika) — a coastal cone guarding the harbour mouth, riddled with historic military tunnels and gun emplacements you can walk through.
  • Mount Wellington (Maungarei) — the largest of the younger cones, with a steep stair climb and a clear crater.

Can you drive to the top of Mount Eden?

No. Vehicle access to the Maungawhau summit was closed in 2016 to protect the maunga and its crater. You park at the lower car park and walk up on formed paths, a climb of about 15 to 20 minutes that most people manage comfortably.

Which cone has the best view?

For the widest view, Maungawhau wins. From the summit you see the full isthmus, both harbours, Rangitoto to the east, and the city skyline. For a harbour-mouth view with something extra to explore, North Head is the pick, with its tunnels and coastal lookouts.

A note on respect for the maunga

Auckland's volcanic cones are not just scenic. The tūpuna maunga are of deep cultural significance to mana whenua and many are co-governed by the Tūpuna Maunga Authority. Stick to formed tracks, keep off the crater rims where signed, and treat them as the living taonga they are.
