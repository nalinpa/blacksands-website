Can You Walk Up Rangitoto and How Long Does It Take?

Yes, you can walk up Rangitoto. The summit track is a 3.1 km return walk that takes about an hour each way, starting from the wharf after a 25-minute ferry from downtown Auckland.

Can you walk up Rangitoto?

Yes. The Summit Track runs from Rangitoto Wharf to the 260-metre peak, climbing through lava fields and the largest pōhutukawa forest in the world. It is open year-round and manageable for most people with moderate fitness. It is not technical, but the track is rough, uneven and slippery with loose gravel in places, so it needs sturdy footwear with good grip. If you would rather not walk, a guided 4WD road train can take you most of the way up.

How long does it take?

About an hour each way at an easy pace, so roughly two hours return. Plan at least three hours on the island so you have time for the lava caves and the summit without rushing. The signposted lava caves turn-off is around 45 minutes along the track, with a short side trail to tunnels left behind when lava cooled on top and kept flowing underneath. Bring a torch if you want to go inside.

How do you get there and what does it cost?

Fullers360 runs the ferry from the downtown Auckland terminal, a 25-minute crossing to Rangitoto Wharf. It costs around 42 NZD return for adults and 22 NZD for children aged 5 to 15, which includes the Department of Conservation landing fee. You can also board at Devonport. Sailings are limited and sell out in summer, so book ahead, and note the last ferry back is usually between 2:30 and 3:30pm. Miss it and there is no way off.

What should you bring?

Everything you need for the day, because there are no shops, cafes or water on the island. Carry plenty of water, as the lava fields get hot and exposed. Wear proper shoes, pack sun protection and a raincoat for the fast-changing weather, and bring a torch for the caves. There are no rubbish bins, so whatever you carry in, you carry out. Rangitoto is pest-free, so check your bags for stowaways and keep food in sealed containers.

What makes Rangitoto special?

It is the youngest volcano in Auckland, formed in eruptions around 600 years ago and witnessed by Māori living nearby. It is also the largest the field has produced. Since a major pest-eradication programme cleared it in 2011, native birds like tūī, bellbird and kākā have returned in force. The summit gives a 360-degree view across the Hauraki Gulf and back to the city.
