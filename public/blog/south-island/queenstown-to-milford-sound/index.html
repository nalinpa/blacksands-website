Queenstown to Milford Sound: Day Trip or Stay in Te Anau?

Stay a night in Te Anau if you can. DOC says Queenstown to Milford Sound is about 4.5 hours each way without stops, a nine-hour return drive before you add a cruise. From Te Anau it's about 240 km return, and the Milford day takes about eight hours with stops and a cruise. A day trip from Queenstown still makes sense on a coach, or on a fly-cruise-fly from about NZ$765 that takes around five hours.

Cruise choice and the planned international visitor access charge, which isn't law and won't apply this summer, live in our Milford Sound guide. This page covers the road. If you're still fitting Fiordland into a wider trip, all three routes in our South Island itinerary include Te Anau and Milford.

When does a day trip from Queenstown still make sense?

A day trip works in three situations. You have no spare night because your flights are fixed. You're on a coach and someone else is driving. Or you're flying both ways and the weather is clear.

Self-driving there and back in a day is the option to avoid. It's about nine hours behind the wheel on a road with one-lane bridges, tunnel queues and coaches. The last two hours from Te Anau back to Queenstown come when you're most tired. You also get one shot at the weather. If the road closes or the cloud sits on the peaks, the day is gone.

Staying in Te Anau halves the Milford driving day. It lets you get through the Homer Tunnel before the Queenstown coaches arrive. Book two nights and you have a spare day if the forecast turns.

What does a day trip from Queenstown look like, hour by hour?

Here's a realistic self-drive day in summer with an early afternoon cruise booked. The times assume short stops and no hold-ups.

  • 6.00am: Leave Queenstown. Te Anau is about 170 km away and Fiordland's own planning page says to allow two hours.
  • 8.00am: Te Anau. Fill the tank here. DOC lists no fuel stations or shops on the 120 km road to Milford. Grab coffee and use the toilets.
  • 8.30am: Leave Te Anau.
  • 9.30am: Mirror Lakes. Ten minutes on the boardwalk.
  • 10.15am: The Divide. A leg stretch only. Key Summit takes three hours and won't fit.
  • 10.45am: Monkey Creek. Five minutes for the valley view and any kea.
  • 11.00am: Homer Tunnel lights. In summer the wait can be up to 20 minutes.
  • 11.30am: The Chasm. Twenty minutes return.
  • 12.15pm: Milford. Park and walk to the terminal.
  • 1.10pm: Cruise. Jucy has listed a 1.10pm summer departure. Most standard cruises run about 1.5 to 2 hours.
  • 3.15pm: Leave Milford. Stop at Pops View on the way back.
  • 5.15pm: Te Anau. Fuel and a quick dinner.
  • 6.00pm: Leave for Queenstown.
  • 8.00pm: Back in Queenstown.

That's 14 hours door to door, with no slack for a slow campervan or a long tunnel queue. The Queenstown coaches leave between 6.45am and 7.35am and take about five hours, so you'll likely share the fiord with them.

How do you spend a night or two in Te Anau?

Te Anau is a small, quiet lakeside town and the last one with facilities before the Milford Road. Evenings are low-key. That's the point: you sleep two hours closer to Milford and start fresh.

Day 1: Queenstown to Te Anau

  • Morning: Leave Queenstown around 9am and you're in Te Anau by about 11am.
  • Late morning: Walk the lakeside track from the Fiordland National Park Visitor Centre to the Control Gates. Fiordland's Te Anau walks page puts it at 50 minutes. On the way you pass Punanga Manu o Te Anau, the bird sanctuary, which is open dawn to dusk for a gold coin donation and has a takahē enclosure.
  • Afternoon: Carry on from the Control Gates onto the Kepler Track. It's a multi-day Great Walk for tramping (hiking), but its first section makes a good day walk. Dock Bay is 1 hour return from the Control Gates. Brod Bay is 3 hours return, or 4 from town. You can pre-book a water taxi to Brod Bay.
  • Evening: Take the Te Anau Glowworm Caves tour with RealNZ, from NZ$154. You cross the lake, then walk and drift by small boat into a glowworm grotto. Fiordland lists it at 2 hours 15 minutes. The cheaper option is Fiordland Cinema, which screens Ata Whenua Shadowland, a film about Fiordland, every day.
  • Before bed: Fill the tank and sort tomorrow's food.

Te Anau is the last town with proper signal, shops and fuel before the Milford Road, so plan tomorrow tonight. Kiwi Guide shows cheap eats, fuel and places to stay near you, so line up dinner, a bed and an early fuel stop while you still have bars.

Day 2: The Milford Road and back

  • 7.00am: Leave Te Anau with a full tank.
  • 8.00am: Mirror Lakes. On a still morning the Earl Mountains reflect in the water.
  • 9.30am: Arrive at Milford and take a mid-morning cruise. The Queenstown coaches won't arrive until around midday.
  • 12.00pm: Head back, stopping at The Chasm, Monkey Creek and Pops View.
  • 1.30pm: Walk to Key Summit from The Divide, about 3 hours return.
  • 4.45pm: Do the Lake Gunn Nature Walk, a 45-minute loop.
  • 6.30pm: Back in Te Anau for a second night, or two more hours to Queenstown.

No car? RealNZ coaches leave Te Anau from 7.45am, from NZ$269, with 2 to 3 hours on the coach each way.

Two nights is better than one. You can pick the clearer of two days for Milford and spend the other on the Kepler.

What are the Milford Road stops, in order?

The distances below are from DOC's Te Anau Milford Highway brochure. Other guides differ by 2 or 3 km, so treat them as rough. Milford is about 120 km from Te Anau. The last flush toilets before Milford are at Knobs Flat, about 62 km out.

  1. Te Anau Downs, about 28 km. Boats leave here for the start of the Milford Track, and it's your first long look up Lake Te Anau.
  2. Eglinton Valley, about 52 km. The road leaves the lake and runs up a wide, flat valley floor between beech forest. Pull over at a signed stop, not on the verge.
  3. Mirror Lakes, about 56 km. A 10-minute boardwalk, wheelchair accessible. On calm days the Earl Mountains reflect in the water.
  4. Lake Gunn, about 76 to 79 km. The Lake Gunn Nature Walk is a 45-minute loop through red beech forest from the Cascade Creek car park at 76 km.
  5. The Divide and Key Summit, about 83 km. At 531 m, DOC calls The Divide the "lowest east-west pass" in the Southern Alps. It's also the start of the Routeburn Track. The Key Summit side trip follows the Routeburn for about an hour, then climbs 20 minutes to a viewpoint at about 920 m. DOC says 3 hours return. Allow longer for the 20-minute summit loop.
  6. Hollyford Valley lookout (Pops View), about 85 km. A roadside view down the Hollyford Valley. Fiordland's road brochure suggests it on the way back.
  7. Monkey Creek, about 93 km. A pull-over with views of the upper Hollyford. You may see whio (blue duck) and kea. Don't feed the kea.
  8. Homer Tunnel, about 99 km. The tunnel is about 1.2 km long, starts 945 m above sea level and drops steeply towards Milford. Cruise Milford's 2026 driving guide puts the grade at about 1 in 10. Traffic lights control it at peak summer times, and DOC warns of waits up to 20 minutes. Use low gear on the way down.
  9. The Chasm, about 110 km. A 20-minute return walk through forest to where the Cleddau River pours through carved rock. A February 2020 storm destroyed the viewing bridges. DOC reopened the walk in April 2023 in a modified form, viewed from the river's edge.

Can you stop near the Homer Tunnel?

Expect very few places. After a rockfall near the tunnel in 2019, NZTA and DOC closed two stopping areas for that summer. These were the Homer Tunnel car park on the eastern side and the Loop 2 car park on the Milford side. They added a no-stopping zone on the approach and for about 850 metres past the western portal. That left The Chasm as the only formal car park on that stretch.

We couldn't confirm which of these stopping areas are open in summer 2026/27, so obey the signs on the day. In avalanche season, the stretch from Falls Creek, above the Hollyford Road junction, to The Chasm is a signposted no-stopping zone.

How do you make the weather call on the morning?

Check the night before and again before you leave. Use these:

  • Road status: NZTA's Milford Road status page and the NZTA Journeys highway conditions map. You can also call 0800 44 44 49.
  • Forecast: MetService's Fiordland National Park forecast.
  • Avalanche updates: NZTA's SH94 Milford Road page explains the avalanche risk ratings and shows the Homer Tunnel weather and a Gertrude Valley webcam.
  • Roadside signs: NZTA keeps road condition signs about 1 km north of Te Anau, at Knobs Flat and near Milford.

Heavy rain isn't a reason to cancel. When it pours, the rock walls above the road and the fiord run with temporary waterfalls. It's arguably the best time to see the place. But rain also brings slips and closures. NZTA's Milford Road FAQs say the road closes about eight days a year, mostly in winter, which NZTA counts as May to October. If the road is shut, coaches can't get through either. Flights have their own weather risk. Glenorchy Air checks conditions an hour before each departure, offers to reschedule and refunds in full if it can't.

Chains can still apply in November. NZTA says you may need to carry them between June and November when signed. Driving past a chains sign without them can earn a NZ$750 police fine. You can hire chains in Te Anau.

Don't count on your phone once you're past Te Anau. DOC says mobile coverage is limited outside town, so make the call before you leave. Our South Island mobile coverage guide covers what to expect.

Frequently asked questions

Can you do Milford Sound as a day trip from Queenstown?

Yes, but it's a long day. DOC puts the return drive alone at about nine hours. RealNZ's coach day trips from Queenstown leave between 6.45am and 7.35am and spend around five hours on the road each way. If you can add a night in Te Anau, the Milford day drops to about eight hours.

How long is the drive from Queenstown to Milford Sound?

Allow about two hours from Queenstown to Te Anau (around 170 km) and another 2 to 2.5 hours from Te Anau to Milford (about 120 km). DOC puts the whole drive at about 4.5 hours each way without sightseeing. With stops and summer tunnel queues of up to 20 minutes, plan on five or more.

Is Te Anau worth staying in?

Yes, mainly for what it saves you. You sleep two hours closer to Milford and get through the Homer Tunnel before the Queenstown coaches. Stay two nights and you also have a spare day if the weather turns. The town itself is quiet. The lakeside walk to the Kepler Track and the bird sanctuary fill an afternoon, and the glowworm caves fill the evening.

Is it better to fly to Milford Sound from Queenstown?

Flying is faster and far more expensive. Fly-cruise-fly trips from Queenstown start from about NZ$765 per person and take around 4 to 5.5 hours. Coach day trips start from about NZ$260 to NZ$320 and take about 12 hours. Both include a cruise. Low cloud can ground flights, so keep a spare day in your plans if you book one.

Where is the last fuel before Milford Sound?

Fill up in Te Anau. DOC lists no fuel stations or shops on the road to Milford Sound, which is about 240 km return. Cruise Milford's driving guide mentions card-only pumps at Milford, but don't rely on them. Bring food and insect repellent too.

Is there phone signal on the Milford Road?

Very little. DOC says mobile coverage is limited outside Te Anau. Check the road status and the forecast before you leave town. Tell someone your plans if you're walking.
